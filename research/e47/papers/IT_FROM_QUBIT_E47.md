Canonical relativistic quantum information theory sets a hard constraint on that ambition: Peres, Scudo and Terno (2002) showed that the von Neumann entropy of a particle's spin is **not a Lorentz invariant**. Under a boost, the Wigner rotation entangles spin with momentum, so the reduced spin density matrix, and its entropy, depend on the observer. Any program that wants geometry to come out of a finite spin register therefore has to say which quantities survive that observer dependence.

# Part I: Results (proved and computationally verified)

## 1. The $V_2^{\otimes 3}$ Representation Space

To achieve algebraic closure without external geometric biases, the system operates natively over the triple tensor product of the spin-2 irreducible representation of $SU(2)$. The carrier space is

$$V = V_2^{\otimes 3} \cong \mathbb{C}^5 \otimes \mathbb{C}^5 \otimes \mathbb{C}^5, \qquad \dim V = 125 .$$

Under the diagonal action of $SU(2)$, generated by $J_a = J_a^{(1)} + J_a^{(2)} + J_a^{(3)}$, the carrier decomposes into isotypic components

$$V_2^{\otimes 3} \cong V_0 \oplus 3V_1 \oplus 5V_2 \oplus 4V_3 \oplus 3V_4 \oplus 2V_5 \oplus V_6 .$$

The total Casimir $C = J_x^2 + J_y^2 + J_z^2$ acts on the spin-$J$ sector as the scalar $J(J+1)$.

| Spin label $J$ | Casimir score $J(J+1)$ | Multiplicity $n(J)$ | Subspace dimension $n(J)(2J+1)$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 2 | 3 | 9 |
| 2 | 6 | 5 | 25 |
| 3 | 12 | 4 | 28 |
| 4 | 20 | 3 | 27 |
| 5 | 30 | 2 | 22 |
| 6 | 42 | 1 | 13 |
| | | **19** | **125** |

**Status: proved and verified.** The census follows from Clebsch–Gordan ($V_2\otimes V_2 = V_0\oplus\cdots\oplus V_4$, then $\otimes V_2$ once more). The script checks it twice: by diagonalizing the $125\times125$ Casimir in float64, and by exact integer weight counting.

## 2. Spectral Moments

Under the normalized trace $\langle X\rangle = \operatorname{tr}(X)/\dim V$, the Casimir spectrum has

$$\mu = \langle C\rangle = 18, \qquad \sigma^2 = \langle C^2\rangle - \mu^2 = 144, \qquad \sigma = 12 .$$

**Lemma (moments of a triple product).** On $V_j^{\otimes 3}$,

$$\mu = 3\,j(j+1), \qquad \sigma = 2\,j(j+1).$$

*Proof sketch.* $C = \sum_i C_i + 2\sum_{i<k} J^{(i)}\!\cdot J^{(k)}$. Each $C_i$ is the scalar $j(j+1)$. The three pair terms are traceless and mutually uncorrelated, and each has second moment $[j(j+1)]^2/3$. So $\sigma^2 = 4\cdot 3\cdot[j(j+1)]^2/3 = [2j(j+1)]^2$. $\blacksquare$

**Status: proved and verified.** The script checks the lemma in exact rational arithmetic for every $j = \tfrac12, 1, \dots, 6$. At $j=2$ it gives $\mu = 18$ and $\sigma = 12$. (Full proof: `research/e47/E47_Moment_Selector_Theorem.md`.)

## 3. The Moment Selector and the E47 Kernel

The one-standard-deviation spectral points are $\mu - \sigma = 6$ and $\mu + \sigma = 30$. Both are Casimir values: $6 = 2\cdot 3$ ($J=2$) and $30 = 5\cdot 6$ ($J=5$). The **moment selector** is the unique monic quadratic polynomial in $C$ that vanishes at $\mu\pm\sigma$:

$$K = (C - 6I)(C - 30I) = C^2 - 36\,C + 180\,I .$$

Uniqueness is elementary, since a monic quadratic is fixed by its two roots. The content of the result is that *both* roots land exactly on the spectrum, so $K$ annihilates whole sectors and is invertible on everything else. On each sector $K$ acts as the scalar $(J(J+1)-6)(J(J+1)-30)$:

| $J$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $K$ eigenvalue | 180 | 112 | **0** | −108 | −140 | **0** | 432 |

So the kernel isolates the stable fixed points:

$$E_{47} = \ker K = \ker(C-6I) \oplus \ker(C-30I) = W(2) \oplus W(5) \cong 5V_2 \oplus 2V_5,$$

$$\dim E_{47} = 5\cdot 5 + 2\cdot 11 = 25 + 22 = 47 .$$

The Universal Coherence Threshold is

$$\Omega_c = \frac{\operatorname{tr}(P_{47})}{\dim V} = \frac{47}{125} = 0.376 .$$

Equivalently, $\Omega_c = \Pr\big[(C-\mu)^2 = \sigma^2\big]$ under the normalized trace. The 47 protected states form the kernel, and the remaining 78 orthogonal states form the complement on which $K$ is invertible.

**Status: proved and verified.** The script checks $\dim\ker K = 47$ numerically, checks $K P_{47} = 0$, and checks $\Omega_c = 0.376$ to $10^{-9}$.

## 4. Convergence to the Kernel: the Richardson Flow

Let $R_\epsilon = I - \epsilon K^2$. Because $K^2 \succeq 0$ commutes with $C$, $R_\epsilon$ fixes $E_{47}$ pointwise and acts on the complement with eigenvalues $1 - \epsilon\lambda$, where $\lambda$ runs over the nonzero spectrum of $K^2$:

$$\operatorname{spec}(K^2)\setminus\{0\} = \{108^2, 112^2, 140^2, 180^2, 432^2\},$$

$$\Delta = \lambda_{\min} = 11664, \qquad \lambda_{\max} = 186624 .$$

The optimal Richardson step and its worst-case contraction factor are

$$\epsilon^* = \frac{2}{\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max}} = \frac{1}{99144}, \qquad
\rho^* = \max_{\lambda}\,\lvert 1-\epsilon^*\lambda\rvert = \frac{\lambda_{\max}-\lambda_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max}+\lambda_{\min}} = \frac{15}{17} .$$

Since $\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min} = 16$, the rate is $(16-1)/(16+1) = 15/17$. The kernel error after $n$ steps satisfies $\lVert e_n\rVert \le (15/17)^n \lVert e_0\rVert$.

**Status: proved and verified by actual iteration.** The script runs $R_{\epsilon^*}$ on a random complex vector. It checks the bound at every step $n = 1,\dots,60$, measures the asymptotic per-step ratio as $0.882352941\ldots = 15/17$, and confirms that the kernel component is unchanged.

## 5. The Fibonacci–Lucas Lemma

The moment rule does not single out $j=2$ by fiat. On any triple product, $\mu - \sigma = j(j+1)$ is always the $J=j$ sector. The upper root $\mu+\sigma = 5j(j+1)$ is a Casimir value exactly when

$$J(J+1) = 5\,j(j+1) \iff (2J+1)^2 - 5(2j+1)^2 = -4 \iff (2j+1,\ 2J+1) = (F_k,\ L_k),\quad k \text{ odd}.$$

Here $F_k$ and $L_k$ are the Fibonacci and Lucas numbers, and $L_k^2 - 5F_k^2 = 4(-1)^k$. For $j \le J \le 3j$, the spin-$J$ multiplicity in $V_j^{\otimes 3}$ is $3j - J + 1$. Combined with $3F_k - L_k = 2F_{k-2}$, this gives

$$n(j) = F_k, \qquad n(J) = F_{k-2}.$$

The two surviving multiplicities are always consecutive odd-index Fibonacci numbers.

| $k$ | $(j, J)$ | $n(j)$ | $n(J)$ | $\dim\ker K_{\text{mom}} / \dim V$ |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| 3 | $(\tfrac12, \tfrac32)$ | 2 | 1 | 8 / 8 (whole carrier) |
| **5** | $(2, 5)$ | **5** | **2** | **47 / 125** |
| 7 | $(6, 14)$ | 13 | 5 | 314 / 2197 |
| 9 | $(\tfrac{33}{2}, \tfrac{75}{2})$ | 34 | 13 | 2144 / 39304 |
| 11 | $(44, 99)$ | 89 | 34 | 14687 / 704969 |

$E_{47}$ is the first member of this family that cuts a proper kernel, and the first with integer spin. It is also the only one in the table whose selected dimension is prime, and the only one where $J$ is the second-highest spin ($3j - J = 1$).

**Status: proved and verified** (exact arithmetic for $k = 3,\dots,11$). Sources: `research/e47/notes/E47_FIBONACCI_LUCAS_LEMMA.md` and `research/e47/E47_Moment_Selector_Theorem.md` in [nicholaskouns-create/E47-Kartekeya](https://github.com/nicholaskouns-create/E47-Kartekeya).

## Open Question

The chain of results now reads

$$\mu \pm \sigma \;\Longrightarrow\; K = (C-6I)(C-30I) \;\Longrightarrow\; E_{47} = 5V_2 \oplus 2V_5,
\qquad
J(J+1) = 5j(j+1) \;\Longrightarrow\; (F_k, L_k).$$

What remains open is the first arrow's premise: **why $\mu \pm \sigma$ is the natural selection rule.** The theorems show which carrier the moment rule forces, and that $V_2^{\otimes 3}$ is its first proper instance. They do not show that the one-standard-deviation rule is the only natural rule. A derivation of that rule, from an extremal principle, a stability criterion, or an information-theoretic argument, is the central open problem of the program.

## 7. Numeric Validation (complete, runnable)

The script below is `research/e47/validation/it_from_qubit_validate.py`. It needs only `numpy`, asserts every Part I statement, and exits 0. (The original edition's title promises PyTorch. Nothing here needs it, and the numpy version is the reference.)

```python

"""
It from Qubit -- E47 numeric validation.

Nicholas S. Kouns, The Kouns-Killion Paradigm | Recursive Intelligence Framework.

Builds the total-spin Casimir C on the carrier V = V_2 (x) V_2 (x) V_2 (dim 125)
and asserts every number stated in the "Results" section of
research/e47/papers/IT_FROM_QUBIT_E47.md:

  * Casimir census  n(J) = 1,3,5,4,3,2,1 ; sector dims 1,9,25,28,27,22,13
  * spectral moments  mu = 18, sigma = 12  (and mu = 3j(j+1), sigma = 2j(j+1))
  * moment selector   K = (C - 6I)(C - 30I),  6 = mu - sigma, 30 = mu + sigma
  * E47 = ker K = 5 V_2 (+) 2 V_5,  dim 47,  Omega_c = 47/125 = 0.376
  * K spectrum per sector 180,112,0,-108,-140,0,432
  * K^2 gap 11664, max 186624, eps* = 1/99144, contraction 15/17 (by iteration)
  * Fibonacci-Lucas lemma  n(j) = F_k, n(J) = F_{k-2}, J(J+1) = 5 j(j+1)

Requires only numpy. Exits 0 when every assertion passes.
"""
from fractions import Fraction as Fr
import sys

import numpy as np

TOL = 1e-9
checks = []


def check(name, ok, detail=""):
    checks.append((name, bool(ok)))
    print(f"[{'PASS' if ok else 'FAIL'}] {name}" + (f"  {detail}" if detail else ""))
    assert ok, name


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- spin matrices
def spin_matrices(j):
    d = int(round(2 * j + 1))
    m = np.array([j - k for k in range(d)], dtype=float)          # j, j-1, ..., -j
    Jp = np.zeros((d, d))
    for i in range(d - 1):                                         # J+ |m> ~ |m+1>
        Jp[i, i + 1] = np.sqrt(j * (j + 1) - m[i + 1] * (m[i + 1] + 1))
    Jm = Jp.T
    Jx = 0.5 * (Jp + Jm)
    Jy = -0.5j * (Jp - Jm)
    Jz = np.diag(m)
    return Jx, Jy, Jz


def total_casimir(j, n=3):
    ops = spin_matrices(j)
    d = ops[0].shape[0]
    I = np.eye(d)
    C = 0
    for A in ops:
        tot = 0
        for site in range(n):
            f = np.array([[1.0]])
            for s in range(n):
                f = np.kron(f, A if s == site else I)
            tot = tot + f
        C = C + tot @ tot
    C = 0.5 * (C + C.conj().T)
    return C


# ---------------------------------------------- exact census by weight counting
def census_exact(j, n=3):
    """Multiplicity of each total spin J in V_j^{(x)n}, exact integers."""
    two_j = int(round(2 * j))
    # weights in units of 1/2: each factor contributes 2m in {-2j, ..., 2j} step 2
    w = {0: 1}
    for _ in range(n):
        nw = {}
        for a, c in w.items():
            for t in range(-two_j, two_j + 1, 2):
                nw[a + t] = nw.get(a + t, 0) + c
        w = nw
    top = n * two_j
    mult = {}
    for twoJ in range(top, -1, -2):
        mult[Fr(twoJ, 2)] = w.get(twoJ, 0) - w.get(twoJ + 2, 0)
    return {J: c for J, c in mult.items() if c}


print("=" * 72)
print("It from Qubit -- E47 validation  (carrier V_2^{(x)3})")
print("=" * 72)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------ 1. carrier
j = 2
C = total_casimir(j)
D = C.shape[0]
check("carrier dimension 125", D == 125, f"dim V = {D}")
check("C is Hermitian", np.allclose(C, C.conj().T, atol=TOL))

evals, evecs = np.linalg.eigh(C)
rounded = np.round(evals).astype(int)
check("spectrum of C is integral", np.max(np.abs(evals - rounded)) < 1e-8,
      f"max dev {np.max(np.abs(evals - rounded)):.1e}")

# --------------------------------------------------------------- 2. census
exp_mult = {0: 1, 1: 3, 2: 5, 3: 4, 4: 3, 5: 2, 6: 1}
exp_dim = {0: 1, 1: 9, 2: 25, 3: 28, 4: 27, 5: 22, 6: 13}
print("\n  J   J(J+1)   mult   dim")
for J in range(7):
    dimJ = int(np.sum(rounded == J * (J + 1)))
    multJ = dimJ // (2 * J + 1)
    print(f"  {J}   {J*(J+1):5d}   {multJ:4d}   {dimJ:3d}")
    check(f"census J={J}: mult {exp_mult[J]}, dim {exp_dim[J]}",
          dimJ == exp_dim[J] and multJ == exp_mult[J] and dimJ % (2 * J + 1) == 0)
check("census sums to 125", sum(exp_dim.values()) == 125 == len(rounded))
check("exact weight-count census agrees",
      {int(k): v for k, v in census_exact(2).items()} == exp_mult)

# --------------------------------------------------------------- 3. moments
mu = np.trace(C).real / D
var = np.trace(C @ C).real / D - mu ** 2
sigma = np.sqrt(var)
check("mu = 18", abs(mu - 18) < TOL, f"mu = {mu:.12f}")
check("sigma = 12", abs(sigma - 12) < TOL, f"sigma = {sigma:.12f}")

print("\n  general triple product V_j^{(x)3} (exact rational, from census):")
for two_j in range(1, 13):
    jj = Fr(two_j, 2)
    cen = census_exact(jj)
    dim = (two_j + 1) ** 3
    m1 = sum(c * (2 * J + 1) * J * (J + 1) for J, c in cen.items()) / dim
    m2 = sum(c * (2 * J + 1) * (J * (J + 1)) ** 2 for J, c in cen.items()) / dim
    ok = (m1 == 3 * jj * (jj + 1)) and (m2 - m1 ** 2 == (2 * jj * (jj + 1)) ** 2)
    print(f"    j={str(jj):>4}: mu={str(m1):>7}  sigma^2={str(m2 - m1**2):>9}")
    check(f"mu=3j(j+1), sigma=2j(j+1) at j={jj}", ok)

# --------------------------------------------------------------- 4. selector
lo, hi = mu - sigma, mu + sigma
check("mu - sigma = 6, mu + sigma = 30", abs(lo - 6) < TOL and abs(hi - 30) < TOL)
# unique monic quadratic with roots lo, hi:  x^2 - (lo+hi) x + lo*hi
coeffs = np.poly([lo, hi])
check("unique monic quadratic vanishing at mu+-sigma is x^2 - 36x + 180",
      np.allclose(coeffs, [1, -36, 180]))
I = np.eye(D)
K = (C - 6 * I) @ (C - 30 * I)

kspec = {J: (J * (J + 1) - 6) * (J * (J + 1) - 30) for J in range(7)}
check("K sector spectrum 180,112,0,-108,-140,0,432",
      [kspec[J] for J in range(7)] == [180, 112, 0, -108, -140, 0, 432])
Kev = np.linalg.eigvalsh(K)
check("numeric K eigenvalues match sector values",
      np.allclose(np.sort(Kev), np.sort([kspec[J] for J in rounded.tolist()
                                          for J in [int(round((-1 + np.sqrt(1 + 4 * J)) / 2))]]),
                  atol=1e-7))

ker_dim = int(np.sum(np.abs(Kev) < 1e-7))
check("dim ker K = 47", ker_dim == 47, f"dim ker K = {ker_dim}")
check("ker K = 5 V_2 (+) 2 V_5", 5 * 5 + 2 * 11 == ker_dim)
P47 = evecs[:, (rounded == 6) | (rounded == 30)]
P = P47 @ P47.conj().T
check("K P47 = 0", np.linalg.norm(K @ P) < 1e-8)
omega = np.trace(P).real / D
check("Omega_c = 47/125 = 0.376", abs(omega - 0.376) < TOL and Fr(47, 125) == Fr(376, 1000),
      f"Omega_c = {omega:.12f}")

# --------------------------------------------------------------- 5. K^2 flow
K2 = K @ K
nz = sorted({v * v for v in kspec.values() if v != 0})
lam_min, lam_max = nz[0], nz[-1]
check("K^2 spectral gap 11664 (=108^2)", lam_min == 11664)
check("K^2 max eigenvalue 186624 (=432^2)", lam_max == 186624)
K2ev = np.linalg.eigvalsh(K2)
check("numeric K^2 gap / max agree",
      abs(np.min(K2ev[K2ev > 1]) - 11664) < 1e-5 and abs(np.max(K2ev) - 186624) < 1e-5)
eps = Fr(2, lam_min + lam_max)
check("eps* = 2/(gap+max) = 1/99144", eps == Fr(1, 99144))
rho = max(abs(1 - eps * v) for v in nz)
check("worst-case contraction max|1 - eps* lambda| = 15/17", rho == Fr(15, 17))

R = I - float(eps) * K2
rng = np.random.default_rng(47)
x = rng.standard_normal(D) + 1j * rng.standard_normal(D)
x_ker0 = P @ x
e = x - x_ker0
e0 = np.linalg.norm(e)
steps = 60
ratios = []
for n in range(1, steps + 1):
    x = R @ x
    e_new = x - P @ x
    ratios.append(np.linalg.norm(e_new) / np.linalg.norm(e))
    e = e_new
    assert np.linalg.norm(e) <= (15 / 17) ** n * e0 * (1 + 1e-9), f"bound violated at n={n}"
check("iteration: ||e_n|| <= (15/17)^n ||e_0|| for n=1..60", True,
      f"||e_60||/||e_0|| = {np.linalg.norm(e)/e0:.3e}, bound {(15/17)**steps:.3e}")
# asymptotic rate: iterate the (renormalized) error e_{n+1} = R e_n on the complement
v = (I - P) @ (rng.standard_normal(D) + 1j * rng.standard_normal(D))
v /= np.linalg.norm(v)
for _ in range(3000):
    w = R @ v
    rate = np.linalg.norm(w)
    v = (I - P) @ w / rate          # (I-P) only scrubs round-off leakage into ker K
check("iteration: observed asymptotic per-step ratio = 15/17",
      abs(rate - 15 / 17) < 1e-9, f"ratio_3000 = {rate:.12f}, 15/17 = {15/17:.12f}")
check("iteration: kernel component preserved", np.linalg.norm(P @ x - x_ker0) < 1e-9)

# --------------------------------------------------------- 6. Fibonacci-Lucas
F = [0, 1]
for _ in range(20):
    F.append(F[-1] + F[-2])
L = [2, 1]
for _ in range(20):
    L.append(L[-1] + L[-2])
print("\n  k   (j, J)          n(j)  n(J)  dim ker K_mom / dim V")
for k in (3, 5, 7, 9, 11):
    jj, JJ = Fr(F[k] - 1, 2), Fr(L[k] - 1, 2)
    cen = census_exact(jj)
    nj, nJ = cen.get(jj, 0), cen.get(JJ, 0)
    sel = nj * (2 * jj + 1) + nJ * (2 * JJ + 1)
    print(f"  {k:<3} ({str(jj)}, {str(JJ)}){'':<{12-len(str(jj))-len(str(JJ))}}"
          f"{nj:5d} {nJ:5d}   {sel} / {(2*jj+1)**3}")
    check(f"F-L k={k}: J(J+1)=5j(j+1), n(j)=F_{k}, n(J)=F_{k-2}",
          JJ * (JJ + 1) == 5 * jj * (jj + 1) and nj == F[k] and nJ == F[k - 2])

# ----------------------------------------------------------------- summary
n_pass = sum(ok for _, ok in checks)
print("\n" + "=" * 72)
print(f"{n_pass}/{len(checks)} checks PASS")
print("E47 = 5V_2 (+) 2V_5, dim 47, Omega_c = 0.376, contraction 15/17: VERIFIED")
print("=" * 72)
sys.exit(0 if n_pass == len(checks) else 1)
```

# Part II: Program and Conjectures

These are the ideas that motivate the Kouns-Killion Paradigm. They are kept here in full, but each is labelled as a conjecture or program item. None of them is established by Part I, and for each one the missing steps are listed.

## Conjecture A: The Unified Reality Equation

$$R := \lim_{n\to\infty} f_n(x) + \int^t dC(t) + \Phi_C\big(V_C(p\vert_{\text{stable}})\big),
\qquad \Phi_C := V_C(p\vert_{\text{stable}}).$$

**Proposal.** $E_{47}$ serves as the incorruptible fixed point of $R$. The limit term is the recursive flow converging to the kernel, the integral accumulates coherence, and $\Phi_C$ is the localized coherence on the stable set.

**Missing steps.**
1. Define $f_n$, $x$, $C(t)$, $V_C$, $\Phi_C$ and $p\vert_{\text{stable}}$ as mathematical objects with domains and codomains. In Part I, $C$ is a fixed operator with no time dependence.
2. The definition $\Phi_C := V_C(p\vert_{\text{stable}})$ makes the third term $V_C(V_C(\cdot))$ or is circular, so it has to be fixed.
3. Show the limit exists. The natural candidate is $f_n = R_{\epsilon^*}^n$, whose limit is $P_{47}$ by §4, and this would be the first rigorous piece.
4. Say which observable $R$ is, and how it is measured.

## Conjecture B: Time as an Information Integral, $T = \int^t dC$

**Proposal.** Time and time curvature are an accumulated quantum-information quantity.

**Missing steps.**
1. A time-dependent quantity to integrate. The Casimir is conserved, so a candidate is the coherence functional $c_n = \operatorname{tr}(P_{47}\rho_n)$ along the flow $\rho_{n+1} = R\rho_n R^\dagger$. Note that $n$ is an iteration count, not physical time.
2. A metric or connection whose curvature $T$ is supposed to measure. The carrier has no manifold on which curvature is defined.
3. A proof that the resulting $T$ is observer-independent, which is where the Peres–Scudo–Terno constraint bites.

## Conjecture C: Spacetime and Gravity Emerge from $V_2^{\otimes 3}$

**Proposal.** Physical spacetime and its transformation groups emerge from the preservation of linear entropy over $E_{47}$. The spin-2 content ($5V_2$) is the seed of the graviton.

**What is already known, and what is missing.** The standard route from spin-2 to General Relativity is the Weinberg–Deser chain. Weinberg (1964, 1965) showed that a *massless* helicity-2 particle in a Lorentz-invariant S-matrix must couple universally to energy-momentum. Deser (1970) showed that requiring a free massless spin-2 field $h_{\mu\nu}$ to couple consistently to its own conserved stress-energy bootstraps to the full Einstein equations. That chain needs field premises that $E_{47}$ does not yet supply:

1. **A Lorentzian 4-manifold or Poincaré symmetry.** $E_{47}$ carries an $SU(2)$ (rotation) action only. The Lorentz group has no nontrivial finite-dimensional unitary representations, so boosts cannot act unitarily on the 125-dimensional carrier. This is the same obstruction Peres et al. identified for spin entropy.
2. **Masslessness and helicity 2.** $V_2$ is a spin-2 representation of $SU(2)$, the little group of a *massive* particle. A massless graviton has little group $ISO(2)$ and two helicities $\pm2$. A map from $V_2$ to helicity states must be constructed.
3. **A local field and gauge invariance** $h_{\mu\nu} \to h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$.
4. **Universal coupling to a conserved $T^{\mu\nu}$**, together with locality and unitarity.

Once 1–4 are supplied, the Einstein equations follow by Weinberg–Deser. Until then, E47 provides a carrier space, and the physics premises are added on top.

**Related work.** Wong (2025, arXiv:2504.03452) is a Gravity Research Foundation essay on the It from Qubit question in quantum gravity. It argues that string theory provides the bulk Hilbert-space factorization needed to define entanglement entropy in a gravitating theory. In the A-model topological string on the resolved conifold, closed strings are factorized into entangled open strings ending on "entanglement branes," and a $q$-deformed quantum trace reproduces the geometric (Gibbons–Hawking-type) entropy. It is relevant context for the emergence program. It does not address finite spin registers, dephasing or cryptography, and the first edition's attribution of a dephasing-immunity result to it has been removed.

## Conjecture D: $U \equiv I$, the Universe Is Information

**Proposal.** The universe and its systems are information.

**Status.** This is a foundational thesis in the line of Wheeler's "It from Bit," not a theorem. It becomes testable only through Conjectures A–C. The program would need to say what observation would distinguish $U \equiv I$ from its negation.

## Program E: Hardware Instantiation, the NULL ITERATION Protocol and 7nm ASIC

**Proposal.** The symbolic $E_{47}$ kernel maps onto recursive-intelligence hardware through the NULL ITERATION protocol, with the Richardson operator $R_\epsilon = I - \epsilon K^2$ as the hardware cycle, and the 0.376 manifold is enforced natively in 7nm FinFET ASIC simulation.

**What is established.** §4 is exactly the arithmetic such a cycle would run: an integer-spectrum operator, step $1/99144$, guaranteed rate $15/17$. All of it is easy to realize in fixed-point logic.

**Missing steps.** A specification of NULL ITERATION. RTL or a cycle-accurate model. The word length and a quantization-error budget showing that the $15/17$ rate and the exact kernel survive finite precision. Simulation or synthesis results (timing, area, power) for the 7nm process. None of these is presented yet.

## Program F: Protected Encoding and Post-Quantum Cryptography

**Proposal.** Securing the $E_{47}$ subspace gives an encoding whose identity is immune to localized and collective dephasing, which would support post-quantum cryptographic encoding.

**A rigorous foothold.** Because $K$ is a function of the total Casimir, $E_{47}$ is invariant under *collective* rotations $U\otimes U\otimes U$. By the theory of decoherence-free subspaces and noiseless subsystems (Zanardi & Rasetti 1997; Knill, Laflamme & Viola 2000), the information protected against collective $SU(2)$ noise lives in the **multiplicity spaces**, of dimension $5$ (for $J=2$) and $2$ (for $J=5$), not in all 47 dimensions. Within each sector the $2J+1$ magnetic states still rotate. This is a concrete, provable direction.

**Missing steps.** (1) A noise model, and a proof of which errors $E_{47}$ corrects or avoids. Local single-site noise is *not* collective and is not automatically suppressed. (2) For cryptography, a computational hardness assumption and a security reduction. A publicly known fixed subspace is an encoding, not a secret, and post-quantum security requires a problem believed hard for quantum adversaries.

## Program G: Dual-Use and Metric-Engineering Architectures

**Proposal.** The invariant coherence transfer of $E_{47}$ supports topological stability for localized energy and metric-engineering architectures for macroscopic operational stability, with dual-use implications.

**Missing steps.** A physical system in which $E_{47}$ is realized. A coupling between that system and a spacetime metric, which requires Conjecture C. A quantitative prediction that can be checked by experiment. Until those exist, this is a long-range aspiration of the program, not a technology claim.
